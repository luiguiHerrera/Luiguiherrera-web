# Strong earnings, selective capital: the market hidden behind the index

Edition of Monday, November 16, 2026

By Luigui Herrera · Market close on Friday, November 13 · editorial cutoff on Sunday, November 15, 2026

## 1. General context

Comparing the November 13 close with October 30, the S&P 500 has barely moved. But stopping at that figure would miss an important part of what happened. The equal-weighted index advanced and, over the past week, all eleven sectors finished in positive territory. At the same time, the Nasdaq continues to lag over the fortnight, while Bitcoin and Ethereum posted double-digit declines. We are not seeing a market moving at one common pace.

The US economy does not offer a simple answer either. According to FactSet, S&P 500 earnings grew 31.9% year over year in the third quarter and 84% of companies beat EPS estimates. Those are strong figures. Yet the aggregate surprise was just 3.1%, versus the five-year average of 7.5%. Companies are doing well, but the market expected a lot. Meanwhile, employment and consumption remain resilient, while inflation and bond yields continue to push up financing costs.

There are three issues I want to observe together without confusing them: earnings growth, market participation and financial conditions. S&P 500 concentration, the profitability of AI investment and dollar strength affect each asset differently. In gold and Japan, I see structural arguments that remain interesting, but do not guarantee strong near-term returns. In Bitcoin and Ethereum, ETF outflows show weakness in an important source of demand, although they do not represent the entire market. Ethereum raises an additional question: if the infrastructure is being used more and more, how much of that growth actually benefits ETH?

### 1.1. Real earnings, demanding expectations

Earnings season has continued to improve. FactSet reports that, by November 13, 91% of S&P 500 companies had reported. Aggregate EPS was growing by 31.9%, 84% had beaten earnings forecasts and 74% had beaten revenue forecasts. But the EPS surprise was 3.1%, well below its five-year average of 7.5%. I also see a somewhat more moderate valuation: the forward P/E fell to 18.3x, versus 18.7x in the previous report. Looking ahead to the fourth quarter, 33 companies had issued negative EPS guidance and 45 had issued positive guidance. [FactSet, Earnings Insight 13/11](https://insight.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_111326.pdf).

My observation is that it is no longer enough to ask whether companies are still making money. We also need to consider how much growth was already priced in and how much investment in data centres and AI infrastructure will ultimately turn into cash. The New York Fed finds that small businesses using AI are more optimistic about revenue and employment, even after accounting for differences between firms. That is interesting and shows favourable expectations, but it does not yet prove that the technology causes greater profitability. [New York Fed, October](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/10/ai-adoption-and-employment-expectations-evidence-from-a-survey-of-small-business-owners/).

### 1.2. Resilient employment and consumption; tighter monetary conditions

The US labour market remains resilient. October added 165,000 jobs and unemployment stood at 4.1%. Meanwhile, CPI rose 0.4% month over month and the final-demand PPI increased 0.3% month over month, with a 6.5% year-over-year change. Household card spending tracked by Bank of America Institute grew 5.8% year over year in October, versus 6.2% in September. It slowed, yes, but did not stop. [BLS employment](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_11062026.pdf) · [BLS CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/CPI.htm) · [BLS PPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_11132026.htm) · [BofA Institute](https://institute.bankofamerica.com/content/dam/economic-insights/consumer-checkpoint-november-2026.pdf).

The [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm) held rates at 3.75–4.00% on October 28, by ten votes to two. However, market borrowing costs rose again: the ten-year Treasury yield moved from approximately 4.98% on October 30 to 5.15% on November 13. The ten-year real yield rose from 2.60% to 2.91%. The FedWatch snapshot we reviewed also favoured a 25-basis-point increase in December. Bear in mind that this represents what futures were pricing at that moment, not a decision already taken by the Federal Reserve. Fed · [Nominal Treasury yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value_month=202611&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve) · [Real Treasury yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value=2026&type=daily_treasury_real_yield_curve).

### 1.3. Flows: stated pessimism, capital still invested

One divergence particularly caught my attention. Bank of America Global Investment Strategy, using EPFR data, records $98,300 million of weekly inflows into global equity funds through November 11. Reuters, by contrast, reports $50,990 million according to LSEG Lipper. These figures should not be added together: the providers and their investment universes differ. And although surveys show pessimism and some hedge funds have reduced technology exposure, that can coexist with substantial fund inflows. What investors say is one thing; how they are positioned is another. [Reuters/Lipper, 13/11](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/global-markets-flows-graphic-2026-11-13/).

### 1.4. Concentration, technology and NVIDIA

Concentration remains one of the S&P 500 issues I pay closest attention to. J.P. Morgan Asset Management calculates that, as of October 7, the ten largest companies represented 41.1% of market capitalisation and 37.5% of trailing earnings. That is a great deal of weight in a small group. However, concentration does not automatically mean overvaluation, nor does it tell us when a correction might arrive. What it does show is how much the index's performance depends on a handful of companies. [J.P. Morgan AM, October](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/CFAI/90e3e413-cf9e-4bb9-84bf-67bafc1111f0/UploadedImages/documents/GrossJared_JPMorgan_20261015.pdf).

Alongside that concentration came a relevant event: [NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition) announced Jensen Huang's departure and appointed Colette Kress as interim CEO, with documentation filed with the SEC between November 11 and 12. This is a company that carries considerable weight in AI expectations, so management continuity matters. On November 15, reports emerged about a possible return by Huang, but I prefer to distinguish news based on sources from a board decision. Earnings were scheduled for November 17, making them another development to watch. NVIDIA · [SEC 8‐K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm) · [Financial Times, 15/11](https://www.ft.com/content/45383d86-33c4-4d99-86e5-5e058d3567c1) · [NVIDIA IR](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Sets-Conference-Call-for-Third-Quarter-Financial-Results/).

## 3. Reading by asset

### 3.1. SPY — earnings, concentration and the cost of capital

#### What happened

SPY finished the fortnight at almost the same level as at the end of October. But it was not a quiet period. There were significant declines and rebounds, while RSP advanced and QQQ finished negative. All eleven sectors gained ground in the final week, especially energy and industrials. Looking only at SPY’s closing level would miss all that movement beneath the surface.

#### What changed

S&P 500 earnings growth continued to reach 31.9% year over year and the forward P/E fell to 18.3x. However, nominal and real bond yields rose, and AI investment continues to require substantial capital. Added to this is the leadership change at NVIDIA. According to J.P. Morgan, the ten largest companies represent around 41% of the index. These are good reasons to avoid assuming that solid earnings alone will be enough to drive the whole market higher.

#### What I expect

I would like to see the improvement in RSP and the other sectors persist, without large technology companies continuing to weaken. I also want to check that companies turn capital expenditure into cash and that credit does not make their financing even more expensive. Higher short interest in QQQ can encourage sharp rebounds, but it is not enough for me to announce a short squeeze.

### 3.2. GLD — structural demand versus tactical pressure

#### What happened

GLD went from $355.78 to $355.45 and, at first glance, it might look as though little happened. However, on November 9 it reached $379.53 and on November 12 it fell to $354.89. In other words, there was a strong rebound followed by a correction that almost erased it. I prefer to look at that path rather than focus on the small change between closes.

#### What changed

The US ten-year real yield rose 31 basis points, from 2.60% to 2.91%, while the dollar strengthened. That raises the opportunity cost of holding an asset that pays no interest. At the same time, the World Gold Council reports 321 tonnes of official purchases in the third quarter and 675 tonnes between January and September. Gold-backed ETFs received $2.7 billion in October, although North America saw outflows while other regions bought. Global holdings reached 4,275 tonnes. Structural demand is present, but investors are not all acting alike. [WGC Q3](https://www.gold.org/news-and-events/press-releases/central-bank-buying-and-etf-inflows-support-gold-demand-q3) · [WGC October ETFs](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/data/global-gold-backed-etf-holdings-and-flows).

Goldman Sachs also adjusted its expectations. According to Kitco’s coverage of a bank note that we do not have in full, it cut its physical gold targets to $4,150 per ounce at the end of 2026 and $4,800 at the end of 2027. This is interesting because the bank has not abandoned the long-term official-buying thesis, but acknowledges that high interest rates make a recovery harder. And remember, these targets are for an ounce of gold, rather than a GLD share. [Kitco / Goldman, November 13](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-11-13/goldman-sachs-cuts-year-end-gold-price-target-4150oz-2027-target-4800oz).

#### What I expect

I remain positive about structural demand, but cautious about the short term. I would like to see real yields stop exerting pressure, the dollar lose some strength and US ETFs regain demand. Central-bank purchases provide important support, although they do not tell us where the bottom is or guarantee that every rebound is sustainable.

### 3.3. FXI — an export surplus and an uneven domestic economy

#### What happened

FXI gained 1.21% between October 30 and November 13, the best result among our six assets. That is a notable difference compared with Bitcoin, Ethereum and Japan. However, I would not assume that Chinese equities as a whole have recovered simply because FXI finished the fortnight positive.

#### What changed

China accumulated a trade surplus of nearly $1.04 trillion between January and October, and its exports grew 35.2% year over year in October. Global AI-related demand is supporting part of that activity. Domestically, however, the economy still shows very different signals: the official manufacturing PMI stood at 49.3, below the expansion threshold, and consumption and property-sector difficulties persist. An economy can export enormous amounts without its households and businesses enjoying the same prosperity. [Reuters, November 10](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-trade-surplus-soars-past-1-trillion-october-exports-jump-352-yy-2026-11-10/).

Looking at FXI’s composition, one point stands out to me. According to BlackRock, on November 10 financials represented 36.71% and consumer discretionary another 24.84%. China Construction Bank and Tencent are among its largest holdings. We have also seen weakness in the CSI 300, but I do not think it is appropriate to automatically apply that performance to FXI. The former represents domestic Chinese shares; our ETF focuses on large companies listed in Hong Kong. These markets and their composition differ. So, beyond the CSI 300, I prefer to follow the earnings and prices of the companies actually held by FXI. [iShares FXI](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239536/ishares-china-largecap-etf).

#### What I expect

To take a more positive view, I need to see concrete improvements in consumption, credit, confidence and earnings, as well as exports. China can keep selling large amounts abroad while its domestic economy remains weak. That distinction matters a great deal for the kinds of companies held in FXI.

### 3.4. EWJ — Japan: solid earnings and a yen that matters

#### What happened

[EWJ](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239665/ishares-msci-japan-etf) lost 1.24% over the fortnight. Here too, the indicators tell different stories: TOPIX declined around 0.59% and the Nikkei 225 about 3.39%. Those two benchmarks are expressed in yen, whereas EWJ trades in dollars and tracks MSCI Japan. I prefer to distinguish their performance because currency or composition changes can explain much of the difference. [JPX TOPIX](https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/markets/indices/realvalues/index.html) · [Nikkei/TOPIX history](https://datosmacro.expansion.com/bolsa/japon) · EWJ.

#### What changed

The Bank of Japan kept its policy rate at 1.25% in October, although it continues discussing how to proceed with monetary normalization. MUFG estimates that USD/JPY could reach 155 at the end of 2026, 153 in the first quarter of 2027, 151 in the second and 149 in the third. These are the bank’s forecasts, rather than current quotes. During our fortnight, the yen weakened against the dollar, reducing the USD value of unhedged Japanese exposure. [BoJ, October outlook](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/outlook/index.htm) · [MUFG, November](https://www.mufgresearch.com/fx/monthly-foreign-exchange-outlook-november-2026).

I find it particularly interesting that even within J.P. Morgan the outlook is not identical. Its asset-management division is overweight TOPIX because of corporate reforms and reflation. By contrast, Private Bank remains neutral on valuation grounds, despite projecting earnings growth of 25% in 2026 and 8% in 2027, with a forward P/E near 16x. The difference does not mean either team is wrong: they work with different mandates and horizons. [JPMAM](https://am.jpmorgan.com/content/jpm-am-aem/global-institutional/us/en/institutional/insights/portfolio-insights/asset-class-views/asset-allocation.html) · [JPM Private Bank](https://privatebank.jpmorgan.com/eur/en/insights/markets-and-investing/asf/finding-opportunities-in-asia-equities-amid-the-selloff).

I also want to remember what we are buying through EWJ. It is not exclusively a semiconductor investment: on November 12, industrials represented 24.07%, financials 19.51%, consumer discretionary 16.19% and technology 15.72%. Its largest holdings include Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (4.65%) and Toyota (4.02%). That is why the effect of Japanese interest rates on banks can matter as much as global chip demand. [BlackRock EWJ](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239665/ishares-msci-japan-etf).

#### What I expect

I would like to check whether earnings revisions remain favorable and whether monetary normalization proceeds smoothly. I will follow the yen, ten-year JGBs, bank margins and industrial investment. A gradual appreciation of the yen could help EWJ in dollars, but an overly abrupt move can also affect exporters and leveraged trades funded in yen.

### 3.5. BTC — ETF outflows and deleveraging

#### What happened

Bitcoin lost 11.68% between the two closes. Beyond the price, the shift in flows looks significant to me. US spot ETFs recorded net outflows of $2.347 billion from November 2 to November 13. Including the October 30 session brings outflows to $2.9902 billion. These are two different periods; I prefer to show both to avoid attributing to November something that began in late October. [Farside BTC](https://farside.co.uk/btc/).

#### What changed

MARA disclosed to the SEC the sale of 13,597 BTC for approximately $1.1 billion after September. The company used $750 million to repay Bitcoin-backed loans and is also developing AI-related digital infrastructure. We should be careful with an explanation that circulated on social media: the proceeds were not all spent directly on data centers. A very significant portion went toward reducing debt. [MARA 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507605/000150760526000028/mara-20260930.htm).

The weekly technical picture is not especially favorable either: Bitcoin lost its 50-week moving average, and MACD and RSI show weakening momentum. These signals help me describe the deterioration, rather than calculate a reliable probability of further declines. The additional decline on Saturday the 14th is an update after the cutoff, so I do not mix it with the changes in the main table.

#### What I expect

To discuss a more convincing recovery, I want to see fewer liquidations, more stable ETF flows and improved liquidity conditions. I will also follow the dollar, real yields and leverage. I do not consider a single rebound session sufficient to declare this pressure over.

### 3.6. ETH — the network grows, but the token has yet to follow

#### What happened

ETH fell from $2,245.58 to $1,929.61, a decline of 14.07% between October 30 and November 13, while Bitcoin lost 11.68%. In other words, Ethereum did more than retreat with the market: it also underperformed BTC again. ETH/BTC moved from approximately 0.02904 to 0.02826 (−2.70%), using the report’s daily closes. A few days of gains in dollars are not enough for me if ETH continues losing ground against Bitcoin. [ETH closes](https://es.investing.com/crypto/ethereum/eth-usd-historical-data) · [BTC closes](https://www.investing.com/crypto/bitcoin/historical-data).

ETF flows do not yet show a convincing change either. Between November 2 and November 13, $764.1 million flowed out, including $398.9 million in the final week. Friday the 13th brought inflows of just $2.3 million. That is better than continuing to lose money, of course, but I still do not see it as a solid recovery signal. Coinbase Institutional also observed an increase of around 30% in ETH sell-order depth on its market in October. That measurement covers October and one specific provider, rather than the entire market on November 13, but it helps show that the pressure began earlier. [Farside Investors, daily series](https://farside.co.uk/ethereum-etf-flow-all-data/) · [Coinbase Institutional, November 2](https://www.coinbase.com/zh-sg/institutional/research-insights/research/trading-insights/crypto-market-positioning-november-2026).

#### What changed

Here I prefer to distinguish Ethereum, the infrastructure, from ETH, the asset we buy. That distinction does change the reading.

Institutional adoption is advancing. J.P. Morgan put JPM Coin (JPMD) into operation on Ethereum L1 on October 19, in addition to Base, and its asset manager had already launched tokenized money-market funds on the network. These are deployed products, rather than announcements of what might happen someday. On November 6, Grayscale estimated that Ethereum held nearly $140 billion in stablecoins and around 40% of tokenized assets, within its study’s sample. This supports Ethereum’s importance as financial infrastructure. [J.P. Morgan Payments](https://www.jpmorgan.com/payments/newsroom/jpm-coin-live-on-ethereum) · [J.P. Morgan AM](https://am.jpmorgan.com/us/en/asset-management/adv/about-us/media/press-releases/jp-morgan-asset-management-launches-second-tokenized-fund-on-ethereum/) · [Grayscale, November 6](https://www.grayscale.com/the-stack/ethereum-is-shaping-the-infrastructure-for-tokenized-finance).

Usage data help explain this. Ethereum Institutional calculates that, between January and October, the mainnet processed an average of 2.17 million daily transactions, 54% more than in the same months of 2025. Layer 2 networks added approximately 23.3 million per day, up 18% within the analyzed sample. On L1, the median transaction cost fell from 35 to 3.5 US cents. Stablecoin transfers recorded cumulative volume of $25.8 trillion (+61%). That sounds extraordinary, but remember: this volume does not equal final payments or new money, because it also includes repeated and internal transfers. [Ethereum Institutional, November 5](https://www.ethereuminstitutional.org/research/state-of-the-ledger).

But here is the point I do not want to overlook: more activity does not automatically mean more value for ETH. The cheaper a network becomes for its users, the less it can collect per transaction. Bitwise observed that average daily fees and MEV fell from 331 to 198 ETH between the second and third quarters of 2026. During that period, L1 transactions fell from 203.85 million to 199.32 million, although they remained above year-earlier levels. That is why I prefer to look at network usage and revenue separately. [Bitwise, Staking Report Q4 2026, p. 5](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/crypto-market-insights/the-bitwise-staking-report-q4-2026).

There is another calculation we cannot skip: how much ETH is issued to reward validators and how much is destroyed through fees. If the network collects less, burning could be insufficient to offset new issuance. I would not call the token deflationary without checking both values over the same period. In addition, Bitwise’s fee and MEV figures do not directly equal ETH burned. [Ethereum.org — supply and issuance](https://ethereum.org/es/what-is-ether/supply/).

Staking also deserves attention. Bitwise counted 43.8 million ETH in validation at the end of the third quarter, equivalent to 35.9% of supply, compared with 33.0% in the previous quarter. But the annualized gross yield fell from 2.81% to 2.63%. Ethereum Institutional reports 44.2 million ETH (36.1%) on October 31. Increasing amounts of capital participate in securing the network, although that does not provide a guaranteed dividend or protect the price against a correction. [Bitwise, October 20](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/crypto-market-insights/the-bitwise-staking-report-q4-2026) · [Ethereum Institutional, November 5](https://www.ethereuminstitutional.org/research/state-of-the-ledger).

#### What I expect

I remain cautious in the short term, but still see interesting medium-term possibilities if concrete evidence emerges. I want to see sustained ETF inflows, an end to the deterioration in ETH/BTC, and infrastructure usage translating into stronger token economics. That means reviewing L1 fees, L2 blob fees, burning versus issuance, and real demand for ETH as collateral and a settlement asset. The Glamsterdam upgrade remains in development: I will not attribute results to it that we do not yet know. [Ethereum.org — Glamsterdam](https://ethereum.org/roadmap/glamsterdam/).

My observation. Ethereum is gaining ground as financial infrastructure. I consider that important, and it is supported by decisions from institutions such as J.P. Morgan. But network growth and token returns are related rather than equivalent. The evidence is still insufficient for me to say that institutional adoption is supporting ETH’s price. That is where the risk lies, and also what I want to see change before taking a more constructive view. Follow-up. I will track ETH/BTC using comparable closes, daily and cumulative ETF flows, L1 and L2 fees, burning versus issuance, staking, and stablecoin and tokenized-asset activity. I will not mix figures from different periods to suggest an improvement that has not yet been confirmed.

## 5. Possible paths — six assets

I do not expect all six assets to react in the same way to the same events. The paths I share are conditions that will help me check whether my reading improves or worsens, rather than forecasts with calculated probabilities.

| Asset | Constructive path: what needs confirmation | Adverse path: what would weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SPY | Breadth across several horizons; solid guidance and cash flow; stable rates and credit | High real yields, more technology pressure, negative EPS revisions |
| GLD | Less pressure from the dollar and TIPS; US ETFs stabilize flows | Further real-yield increases, a strong dollar and deleveraging |
| FXI | Earnings, consumption and credit recovery in Hong Kong/China | Weak PMI, property risk, export dependence |
| EWJ | Broad earnings, reforms, a predictable BoJ and an orderly yen | Disorderly JGB and yen moves, weakening AI and consumption |
| BTC | ETFs stop losing capital, liquidations ease and liquidity improves | Persistent redemptions, corporate sales and technical pressure |
| ETH | Sustained ETF inflows, ETH/BTC relative strength and greater economic value capture (fees, net burning and collateral demand) | Outflows and relative weakness continue; L2 networks grow without a proportional increase in value captured by ETH |

A favorable path for one asset can be adverse for another. Falling yields, for example, could benefit gold, but not necessarily SPY if accompanied by deteriorating credit. A stronger yen can improve EWJ’s dollar value, although an overly abrupt appreciation could hurt exporters. I prefer to observe each scenario through its own drivers, rather than assume the whole market will move together.

Options: implied move from a complete ATM straddle, before the announcement. It implies neither direction nor a calibrated success rate.

Scenarios: the paths express conditions that can be confirmed or weakened, rather than calculated probabilities or price levels for executing trades.
