AI keeps growing, but markets no longer look only at investment amounts. Who can finance growth, who turns it into cash flow and how long the economy can withstand high long-term yields are becoming important.

## General context

The market enters September with a clear contradiction: equities remain strong, but long-term bonds are beginning to set the limit.

Morgan Stanley’s August Global Reflections focuses on earnings, EPS and free cash flow. Nomura’s GET WONKY connects long-term yields to debt supply and AI financing. Our reading is that the cost of financing growth deserves as much attention as growth itself.

Our AI reading focuses on who can finance investment in chips and data centers, and who can turn it into cash flow. Spending alone does not answer that question.

In August’s FMS, BofA describes low cash holdings, high equity exposure and semiconductor concentration. J.P. Morgan argues that some of software’s punishment could overstate AI disruption. In our reading, both ideas call for distinguishing among companies.

Our gold reading combines flows with debt and diversification away from the dollar. In China, valuation appeal is an editorial hypothesis awaiting confirmation from consumption. In Japan, it matters to separate equities, the yen and the Bank of Japan.

The starting reading remains constructive but more demanding: markets can keep rising while earnings and cash flow offset high yields. If bonds begin pressuring credit and breadth, the reading changes.

September does not begin with a growth problem. It begins with a question about how much financing it costs.

## Possible paths

### Scenarios

#### Base path · the market absorbs high yields and keeps rotating

The S&P 500 retains a functional structure while earnings and cash flow offset high yields. DXY stays orderly; gold retains demand without ruling out pauses. China needs consumer confirmation and Japan absorbs the yen/BoJ relationship. BTC and ETH depend on liquidity remaining open. In AI, financing investment and monetizing it become more important; company dispersion continues.

#### Favorable path · inflation eases, bonds find buyers and the rally broadens

More contained inflation reduces pressure on yields and DXY without signaling a growth contraction. S&P 500 breadth improves and gold retains support with a lower opportunity cost. Firmer Chinese consumers and an orderly yen/BoJ adjustment help Asia. More complete, positive flows favor BTC and ETH’s relative recovery. Software starts confirming better conversion of usage into cash flow.

#### Adverse path · yields break the balance and the cost of capital finally reaches equities

Long-term yields rise and credit becomes more expensive. The S&P 500 loses breadth and rotation becomes reduced exposure. Stronger DXY could pressure gold and China; gold could also receive defensive demand, so its reaction is not automatic. Japan adds tension if the yen and BoJ surprise. BTC and ETH suffer if liquidity falls. AI companies most dependent on external financing, and software companies with insufficient cash flow, would have less room.

These are the editor’s scenarios rather than forecasts by the cited institutions. They help identify which combination of signals is gaining weight as September progresses.
