## General context

### A stronger market than July suggested

**Hedge funds rebuilt US exposure and buying broadened beyond large technology companies.**

The first half of August left the US market stronger than late July’s shakeout suggested. Hedge funds rebuilt US exposure and buying extended beyond large technology companies, a favorable signal for market breadth.

### Rotation within the market, rather than capital withdrawal

**Several momentum leaders weakened while lagging companies recovered ground.**

The improvement nevertheless has a different composition. Several previous momentum leaders weakened while lagging sectors and companies recovered. Through the cutoff, this fits rotation within the market better than a general withdrawal of capital.

### Positioning is more demanding

**Bank of America’s Bull & Bear indicator reached 9.7 out of 10, an area of extreme optimism.**

Positioning is also more demanding. Bank of America’s Bull & Bear indicator reached 9.7 out of 10, a level the bank considers extreme optimism. This does not predict a decline by itself, but leaves less room for disappointment to pass unnoticed.

### The artificial-intelligence bill

**The market is starting to examine how expansion is financed, as well as how much is invested.**

AI continues to attract investment, but the market is beginning to examine a second variable: how that expansion is financed. Increased debt issuance associated with data centers and infrastructure, alongside higher credit premiums at several large technology companies, suggests that the AI cycle’s financial cost is becoming as important as expected growth. If more cash flow goes toward investment and financing, buybacks may also provide less marginal support than in previous years.

### Gold regains demand and approaches resistance

**Systematic demand has returned, but an important technical area still lies ahead.**

Gold regained systematic buyers after its correction, but is approaching an important technical area. This is less straightforward than simply bullish or bearish: demand has returned, but prices must still demonstrate an ability to cross significant resistance.

### What will determine the second half of August

**Long-term yields and DXY again cut across assets ahead of PCE, Jackson Hole, OPEX and earnings.**

For the second half of the month, long-term yields and DXY again affect multiple assets. PCE, Jackson Hole, monthly options expiry and FUTU and NVIDIA earnings can change expectations for rates, growth and AI spending. The starting picture is therefore a market that still has reasons to remain functional, but less room for mistakes.

## Reading by asset

### S&P 500

**The correction did not turn into a general liquidation: the market found new buyers.**

Classification: **functional**

- **What happened:** After late July’s risk reduction, hedge funds increased US exposure again. Morgan Stanley showed US buying that extended beyond technology into materials, healthcare, real estate and other parts of the economy. The index regained strength while intense rotation took place beneath the surface: several former momentum leaders declined and previously punished companies rebounded. At the August 14 close, sector participation was broad—9 of 11 sectors positive and 8 of 11 above their long-term average in the dashboard—although equal weight still failed to outperform the capitalization-weighted index.
- **What changed:** The main difference from the first report is that the correction did not develop into a general liquidation. At the same time, the risk changed shape. BofA’s Bull & Bear at 9.7/10, violent momentum rotation and the increasing cost of financing AI infrastructure reduce tolerance for disappointment. The discussion now extends beyond technology growth: debt, credit, long-term rates and cash-flow allocation are becoming more important.
- **What I expect:** The base case remains compatible with a functioning S&P 500, but greater sector and company dispersion. Continuation would be healthier if breadth holds and leadership keeps broadening. A sharp rise in yields and DXY, simultaneous deterioration in breadth and credit, or a major disappointment from large technology companies would increase the risk of rotation becoming a broad reduction in exposure.

### Gold

**Systematic buyers have returned, but technical confirmation still matters.**

Classification: **awaiting confirmation**

- **What happened:** After a correction of around 27% from its highs, gold rebounded and attracted systematic demand again. According to Charlie McElligott’s analysis, the CTA signal moved from roughly -18% short to +15% long, with an estimated $1.5 billion of buying during the reversal. BTIG placed an important resistance zone around $4,400–4,500, while the dashboard showed GLD flow pressure consistent with likely net inflow on August 14: +0.03% over 1D, +0.59% over 5D and +2.49% over 20D.
- **What changed:** The tactical thesis improved: it is no longer only a structural narrative about debt or diversification; systematic buyers have returned and GLD flows improved. Technical confirmation still matters, however. McElligott also identifies a conditional level near $5,056 that could, under his model, produce a much larger CTA long exposure. This is not a price target.
- **What I expect:** The scenario improves if gold absorbs the $4,400–4,500 zone without simultaneous dollar or real-yield strength. Rejection at resistance alongside a stronger dollar and higher yields would favor consolidation or retracement. A sustained break, especially with DXY and yields contained, could attract further systematic demand.

### China

**Strong exports and weak domestic demand: the contrast became more explicit.**

Classification: **tactical**

- **What happened:** China’s economy continued to show a clear gap between external strength and domestic weakness. July exports grew around 24% year over year and the trade surplus reached roughly $113 billion, while second-quarter GDP growth was 4.3% and retail sales had shown a much weaker tone. Beijing maintained selective support and accelerated already-budgeted spending without announcing a large new consumer-stimulus program. August seasonality provides no useful directional signal on its own: FXI finished August positive in 5 of the last 10 years.
- **What changed:** The broad Chinese market’s resilience during technology pressure remains relevant, but the contrast between strong exports and weak domestic demand became more explicit. This limits an overly optimistic reading based only on valuations or policy. The export engine is strong; domestic consumers have yet to provide equivalent confirmation.
- **What I expect:** The reading remains tactical and mixed. Clear improvements in confidence, consumption or stimulus targeting domestic demand would improve the quality of a recovery. Conversely, stronger DXY, greater trade tensions or persistent domestic weakness would sustain dispersion and limit a stronger directional thesis.

### Japan

**The yen returned as the central variable in the Japanese reading.**

Classification: **selective**

- **What happened:** Japan continued attracting institutional interest within the global rotation, but the yen again became central. After coordinated intervention moved the yen from a low near 163.99 per dollar to around 155.20, it weakened again toward 159.5 by August 14. The US–Japan yield differential remained wide and the market increased its bets on another Bank of Japan increase in September.
- **What changed:** Yen weakness no longer serves only as support for exporters. Approaching 160 again also increases the risk of intervention, imported inflation and a faster monetary response. Japan’s reading therefore depends more than at the start of the month on the interaction among earnings, currency and monetary policy.
- **What I expect:** Relative strength could continue if yen weakness stays orderly and the Bank of Japan normalizes rates gradually. Renewed currency intervention or faster tightening could strengthen the yen and increase sector dispersion, especially among export-sensitive companies.

### Bitcoin

**No clear upward breakout, with ETF flows still mixed.**

Classification: **high beta**

- **What happened:** Bitcoin reached the cutoff around $63,000 and failed to turn somewhat softer macro data into a clear upward breakout. On August 14, the dashboard showed a still-mixed spot ETF flow signal: latest flow -123 M USD, -229 M USD over five days and a two-day outflow streak.
- **What changed:** The reading rests less on a directional narrative and more on the combination of liquidity, DXY, yields and ETF flows. Reliable information through the cutoff does not justify a stronger independent thesis.
- **What I expect:** A more contained dollar and yields, alongside persistent improvement in ETF flows, would favor a more constructive environment. Tighter financial conditions or renewed sustained outflows would keep Bitcoin a high-beta asset sensitive to global risk appetite.

### Ethereum

**Insufficient independent institutional evidence to separate it from the general crypto regime.**

Classification: **high beta**

- **What happened:** Ethereum shared crypto’s volatility and liquidity sensitivity. Specific institutional information available through the cutoff is scarcer than for Bitcoin and provides no sufficiently strong independent signal to confidently separate its direction from the general crypto regime.
- **What changed:** No new independent thesis invalidates the previous reading: DXY, rates, liquidity and risk appetite remain the main macro filters.
- **What I expect:** The scenario improves if financial conditions ease and crypto regains flows and participation. If the dollar and yields rise or risk appetite weakens, ETH would remain vulnerable. Relative strength against BTC should not be inferred without a specific source.

### DXY

**A cross-asset factor: it can simultaneously amplify or ease pressure on gold, China and crypto.**

Classification: **cross-asset factor**

- **What happened:** The dollar continued transmitting interest-rate differences and monetary policy into other assets’ performance. Yen tensions showed that yield differentials retain the ability to move currencies even after official intervention.
- **What changed:** For the second half of August, PCE and Jackson Hole increase DXY’s sensitivity to changing Fed expectations. Its role is more important because it can simultaneously amplify or ease pressure on gold, China and crypto.
- **What I expect:** More persistent inflation or a more restrictive Fed would tend to support the dollar and yields. More contained inflation combined with greater confidence in disinflation could produce the opposite move. DXY is used here as a cross-asset factor, rather than a recommended asset.

## Possible paths

These paths are not predictions. They help identify which combination of signals is gaining importance as the second half of the month progresses.

### Scenarios

#### Base path — a functioning market with rotation

The S&P 500 retains a reasonably firm structure, but leadership keeps rotating. Breadth prevents general selling, yields and DXY remain orderly, and gold tests resistance without an immediate regime break. PCE and Jackson Hole create volatility but do not abruptly change monetary expectations. NVIDIA confirms strong AI infrastructure demand, although the market pays increasing attention to its financing cost.

#### Favorable path — breadth, disinflation and less rate pressure

More contained inflation and less restrictive communication ease pressure on yields and DXY. S&P 500 participation keeps broadening and rotation stops depending on a few leaders. NVIDIA validates enough growth and margin strength to sustain AI spending without worsening financing concerns. Gold clears resistance with systematic-buyer support, and liquidity conditions favor improving crypto flows.

#### Adverse path — rates and the dollar turn rotation into risk reduction

Inflation or Fed communication forces markets to price higher rates for longer. Yields and DXY rise, credit spreads widen and weakness extends beyond former leaders. The S&P 500 loses breadth, gold fails to hold above resistance against a stronger dollar, and BTC/ETH suffer further outflows or weaker risk appetite. An NVIDIA disappointment could amplify the revision to AI expectations.
