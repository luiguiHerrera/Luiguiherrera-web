## General context

In the first October report I focused on a clear divergence: the S&P 500 remained relatively firm, but fewer companies participated in the advance. Two weeks later, the picture changed. **Breadth improved while several of the largest technology companies fell sharply.**

Between October 2 and October 16, SPY fell 0.83% and QQQ lost 5.31%. RSP—which gives similar weight to S&P 500 companies—gained 2.08%. Less favorably, IWM, our small-company reference, fell 3.94%. There is rotation, but not a broad recovery. [[M1]](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-spy/historical/)

The question occupying my attention now is: **is capital finding new opportunities outside large technology companies, or are we seeing the beginning of a broader correction?** Breadth provides a relevant signal, but cannot answer that question alone.

Added to this is a demanding macro environment. September CPI reached 3.7% year over year and PPI 6.3%. The Federal Reserve’s target rate remains 3.75–4.00%. Nevertheless, the US ten-year yield fell between our cutoffs. This combination allows different explanations: safe-haven demand, positioning adjustments and expectations about growth and monetary policy. It does not imply that the Fed has begun easing financial conditions. [M2–M5]

**I retain a constructive fourth-quarter reading, with greater selectivity.** Improved breadth is concrete evidence; now we must check whether it persists and whether corporate earnings justify earnings and investment expectations, especially in AI. Greater participation without prolonged losses among leaders would improve the picture. If the technology correction reaches credit and currently resilient sectors, we will need to review that reading.

### Breadth and rotation: a real but incomplete improvement

In the latest five-session window, **7 of 11 sector ETFs** finished positive versus 3 of 11 previously. Consumer Staples (XLP, +5.02%), Energy (XLE, +4.86%) and Healthcare (XLV, +3.45%) were among the strongest, while Technology (XLK) lost 7.70%. Between reports, XLV gained 6.34%. Returns use one consistent window and can differ from publications using other dates. [[M1]](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-spy/historical/)

This behavior has two sides. RSP recovering against SPY indicates less concentration in the largest companies. However, **IWM has yet to confirm that recovery**. RSP represents equally weighted large companies; IWM follows small companies. They are not equivalent, as October’s results make clear.

Ryan Detrick highlighted that 378 S&P 500 constituents rose on October 15 and that nine of the previous eleven sessions had more advancers than decliners. These data support improved participation on October 15 rather than describing Friday, October 16’s close. [A1]

### Inflation, bonds and financing: pressures we must distinguish

The most relevant macro release was September inflation. **Headline CPI rose 0.6% month over month and 3.7% year over year**; gasoline explained more than half of the monthly increase. Core CPI rose 0.3% monthly and 2.6% annually. The following day brought **PPI**, up 1.3% monthly and 6.3% annually, largely driven by energy. These are different indices and should not be mixed. [M2–M3]

Nominal retail sales grew **0.8% month over month**. This signals activity, but does not mean real consumption increased proportionally. Meanwhile, Michigan showed sentiment falling from 48.1 to 46.3 and one-year inflation expectations rising from 4.6% to 4.7%. For now, spending holds up better than confidence. [M4, M9]

Despite inflationary pressure, the US ten-year Treasury yield fell from **5.28% to 5.09%** between October 2 and October 16, a decline of **19 basis points**. I would not attribute the whole move to one cause. Defensive buying, position hedging and changing expectations can all contribute. Financing costs nevertheless remain high for heavily indebted businesses and some smaller companies. [[M5]](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value=2026&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve)

### Sentiment and positioning: fear does not mean already sold

CNN Fear & Greed reached **18, extreme-fear territory**, at the October 16 snapshot close. AAII recorded 41.6% bears. At the same time, the October 15 NAAIM snapshot was around **74.53**, showing considerable equity exposure among that sample’s active managers. [A2, M10]

Fear & Greed combines market signals; AAII asks surveyed investors what they think; NAAIM measures declared exposure. These are three different thermometers. **Extreme fear does not mean everyone has sold, or automatically turn a decline into a buying opportunity.**

Concentration also persists. Reproductions of Bank of America’s manager survey show net technology overweight reaching **37%**, from September’s 30%. Consumer Discretionary, by contrast, was heavily underweight. BofA Bull & Bear fell to **7.4**: that model’s sell signal switched off, but positioning remained far from defensive. These are BofA’s figures and criteria rather than this report’s own indicators. [A3, M11]

Flows confirm that not all capital was leaving equities. In the week ended October 14, US equity funds received **$13.01 billion net**, although technology funds saw **$5.21 billion** of outflows. US bond funds attracted **$6.45 billion**. Together, this fits **risk redistribution** better than a general market exit. [[M12]](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-equity-funds-draw-renewed-inflows-treasury-yields-ease-2026-10-16/)

### Artificial intelligence: a good industry does not make all its companies good investments

The technology correction was visible even among familiar names. In the weekly sample, Broadcom (AVGO) lost 9.07%, Tesla (TSLA) 7.31% and Nvidia (NVDA) 6.61%. Apple (AAPL) was an exception, rising 2.03%. These changes refer to that weekly window rather than the full return between reports. Nor do I attribute each move to one news item. [A4]

However, **falling share prices do not demonstrate that AI demand has ended**. The cited J.P. Morgan research excerpt presents a scenario of strong data-center investment and HBM and DRAM memory supply constraints. That could continue benefiting specific chip and equipment manufacturers. But its estimates are projections rather than realized revenue or guaranteed returns. [A5]

Credit sits on the other side. CoreWeave’s shares came under pressure and its bonds traded at high double-digit yields. When capital becomes more expensive, a company needing to refinance debt has less room than a large hyperscaler able to invest from its own resources. **Demand can be solid while certain financing structures remain fragile.** [[M13]](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-10-16/)

In *stock picking*, I therefore want to distinguish three groups: suppliers with contracts, orders and pricing power; large buyers able to turn investment into earnings and cash flow; and heavily leveraged operators dependent on future refinancing. Upcoming earnings must clarify margins, capex, depreciation and cash flow. Investment announcements alone do not confirm business profitability.

### Oil and energy: a risk we should keep in view

During the week, Brent approached **$110 per barrel** amid geopolitical tensions and supply difficulties. The October 9 J.P. Morgan note excerpt identifies a particular issue: even when oil is available, tanker-fleet capacity and utilization can make transport more expensive. This is a relevant explanation, but does not establish the cause of every price move. [A6, M14]

Rising oil creates winners and losers. It can boost energy businesses while pressuring inflation, consumption and other industries’ margins. Strong XLE performance does not necessarily make the move favorable for the whole market.

## Reading by asset

### S&P 500 · SPY

SPY fell from **$769.64 to $763.25**, down **0.83%** between reports. Meanwhile, RSP gained 2.08% and QQQ lost more than 5%. This is an unusual combination: internal participation improves without the main index advancing. [[M1]](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-spy/historical/)

A decline of less than 1% between two closes does not invalidate an uptrend, but also fails to describe the adjustment suffered by several large technology companies. The fourth quarter retains a favorable history; the test now is whether improved breadth lasts through earnings season.

More constituents recovering their 50- and 200-day averages, stability in technology and improving IWM relative to SPY. If RSP, SPY and breadth deteriorate together, I will adopt a more defensive stance.

### Gold · GLD

GLD went from **$380.14 to $382.01** (**+0.49%**). It held up better than QQQ, although this small change is insufficient to describe an extraordinary defensive-capital inflow. [[M1]](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-spy/historical/)

I still see structural arguments for gold, including diversification and monetary uncertainty. That does not make it immune to corrections. The dollar, real yields and profit-taking also matter short term. A brief coincidence between falling bond yields and rising gold does not demonstrate a fixed causal relationship.

Gold ETF flows and holdings, dollar behavior, real yields and the metal’s ability to withstand further pressure. Without confusing physical-gold prices with exact GLD ETF levels.

### China · FXI

FXI advanced from **$33.19 to $33.84** (**+1.96%**), outperforming SPY between cutoffs. [[M1]](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-spy/historical/)

This relative improvement is worth following. But two weeks of gains do not prove that liquidity measures are driving consumption and earnings. FXI also mainly holds large Chinese companies listed in Hong Kong: it does not represent the entire economy or Chinese equity market.

Sustained price and earnings improvement, domestic-demand signals, credit developments and measurable economic-policy effects. Without confirmation, an FXI gain cannot be extrapolated into recovery across China’s economy.

### Japan · EWJ

EWJ declined from **$98.92 to $94.88** (**−4.08%**), one of the largest falls among our tracked assets. [[M1]](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-spy/historical/)

Japan is exposed to the global industrial and technology cycle, its own interest rates and yen behavior. EWJ trades in dollars and is therefore not equivalent to measuring only the Nikkei in local currency.

Japanese equities, the yen, Bank of Japan policy and exporter and semiconductor earnings. A convincing recovery would need more than a favorable currency move.

### Bitcoin · BTC

Using CoinGecko daily UTC closes, BTC went from approximately **$84,506 to $81,524**, down **3.53%** between October 2 and October 16. This timing does not precisely coincide with Wall Street’s close. [[M15]](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/bitcoin/historical_data)

The decline accompanies a difficult technology period but does not establish permanent correlation. The previous report proposed selective accumulation. I consider that hypothesis neither confirmed nor invalidated without reviewing new address cohorts, ETF flows and spot demand.

Spot buying, five-session ETF flows and price recovery with broader demand. I would not publish a precise buying zone without updated statistical levels calculated in-house.

### Ethereum · ETH

ETH went from approximately **$2,667.95 to $2,420.56** (**−9.27%**). It fell substantially more than Bitcoin and ETH/BTC lost around **5.95%** between cutoffs, using the same UTC series. [[M15]](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/bitcoin/historical_data)

This shows relative weakness. If Ethereum rebounds against the dollar but keeps losing against Bitcoin, an important confirmation remains missing. Its volatility demands particular care in drawing conclusions.

Sustained ETH/BTC improvement, network economic activity, investment flows and spot volume. Price alone is insufficient.

### Stock picking · Technology and AI

The correction widened differences among companies that previously seemed to move together. Apple held up relatively well in the weekly sample, while other major names and more fragile financing structures suffered significant declines. This does not establish a single reason for Apple acting as a haven or imply that all AI has lost potential. [A4–A5]

**Here I want to focus the analysis on business quality:** orders turning into revenue, sustainable margins, cash generation, real contractual commitments and the capacity to finance growth without relying on exceptionally favorable credit conditions.

In reproductions of BofA’s analysis, Michael Hartnett favors heavily underweight areas such as Consumer Discretionary and sees small-company and real-estate opportunities if yields decline. This is an interesting but still-conditional thesis. IWM’s fall reminds us that a valuation opportunity does not equal a confirmed recovery. [A3]

## Seasonality and upcoming events

The first report covered historically favorable October and fourth-quarter behavior. That precedent provides context rather than a new signal. Carson’s studies of midterm-election years show major corrections and strong subsequent returns from historical lows. The detail is that those lows are identified *after* they occur. **We cannot use that statistic to guarantee that the 2026 low is already behind us.** [A7]

### Halloween: an interesting pattern rather than an automatic trade

Between 1993 and 2025, the late-October/early-November window recorded **24 positive and 9 negative years**, a **72.7%** success rate and a simple average return of **+1.67%**. In the 1993–2022 sample, **76.7%** of years were positive—**23 of 30**. These are different periods rather than two estimates for one sample. [A8]

The consistency test lies in the more recent sample: between **2016 and 2025**, only **6 of 10 years** were positive and the average return falls to **+0.50%**. Removing 1997 and 2008, two extraordinary outcomes, also reduces the 33-year average to approximately **+1.07%**. This does not prove the pattern disappeared, but shows that its historical advantage depends on the chosen sample.

Calculations use rounded returns. Prices, dividends, costs, exact dates and window selection have not been independently verified. The **October 27–November 4** window also crosses the Fed meeting and US elections. I therefore retain it as a **statistical reference rather than an invitation to trade SPY or options**.

## The three scenarios I am following

### Base case · more participation, still with volatility

My base case allows some breadth improvement to persist without a linear S&P 500 recovery. Technology will remain sensitive to earnings and valuations, while healthcare, staples, energy and some lagging companies could support equal weight. IWM will probably need better financing conditions to participate. **This is a working hypothesis rather than a calculated probability.**

### Favorable case · earnings confirm recovery

Leaders deliver earnings and forecasts capable of justifying their investment. Perceived AI infrastructure credit risk improves, bonds avoid abrupt yield increases and recovering sectors retain their gains. RSP keeps improving, participation above MA50 and MA200 increases, and IWM begins joining in. The advance would then depend less on a few companies.

### Adverse case · the correction spreads

The risk is that AI valuation and debt doubts spread into other sectors. If credit becomes more expensive, earnings disappoint or inflation pressure raises required yields again, rotation could cease being healthy redistribution and become broader selling. The deterioration signal I would watch most closely is a simultaneous decline in **SPY, RSP, IWM and breadth measures**. In that environment I would not assume protection from gold or cryptocurrencies either.

| **SPY and breadth** | Sustained improvement above MA50/MA200 and RSP/SPY strength | A simultaneous decline in the index and participation |
| **QQQ and semiconductors** | Strong orders, margins and guidance; stabilizing leadership | Technical rebounds without earnings support |
| **Small companies · IWM** | Relative recovery and better financing conditions | Renewed weakness against SPY and RSP |
| **Inflation, bonds and oil** | An orderly yield decline without further price pressure | Persistent inflation, expensive oil and tighter credit |
| **AI financing** | Sustainable capital access and capex converted into cash flow | Persistent spread and refinancing deterioration |
| **Gold · GLD** | Flows and demand withstanding corrections | Persistent outflows and lost demand |
| **China · FXI** | Earnings and consumption improvement accompanying liquidity | Gains without economic confirmation |
| **Japan · EWJ** | Equity recovery and yen stability | Simultaneous deterioration in equities, currency and financing |
| **Bitcoin · BTC** | Spot demand and verifiable inflows | Selective accumulation insufficient against broader selling |
| **Ethereum · ETH** | Sustained improvement against BTC and in network activity | A dollar rebound without relative strength |
| **Stock picking** | Profitable growth, margins and cash flow per share | Plenty of narrative and little return on capital |

The market shows internal improvement absent two weeks ago, but that does not mean risk disappeared. The decisive factor will be neither an isolated rebound nor a seasonal pattern: it will be whether participation persists and earnings justify current valuations. **Until those conditions are confirmed, I retain a constructive, selective reading without jumping to conclusions.**
