## Main thesis

The semiconductor and momentum correction was extraordinary, but does not yet amount to a broad market breakdown. It combined sector news, crowded positions and leveraged vehicles; meanwhile, long-term yields and large technology companies’ credit spreads raised the cost of financing AI expansion. August can amplify moves in both directions: the base case remains a functioning index with high dispersion, conditional on earnings, margins and cash flow beginning to justify spending.

## General context

### S&P 500: index and internal structure

**Aggregate S&P 500 performance hid much more violent rotation beneath the surface.**

Volatility first concentrated in momentum, semiconductors and AI infrastructure. Correlation among S&P 500 stocks stayed near multi-decade lows, allowing winners and losers to offset one another within the index. Low correlation is not bearish in itself: risk emerges if correlation rises alongside VIX and deteriorating breadth.

### Semiconductors and China

**The news changed perceived risk before industrial capacity changed.**

Different institutional series put the five-session decline in Asian semiconductors at approximately 9%–12%, depending on the universe used. The trigger was China’s announced advance in DUV lithography, but the sources do not yet demonstrate commercial-scale production, sustained industrial yields or EUV capability. The shock was sector-specific: the broad Chinese index did not fall with the same intensity.

### AI, credit and cash flow

**The market stopped rewarding spending alone and began demanding returns.**

AI’s test shifted from how much large technology companies invest to how much revenue, margin and cash flow that investment produces. Credit spreads for several major AI financiers widened against peers, signaling that bond markets are starting to charge more to sustain CapEx. This does not confirm a crisis, but reduces tolerance for disappointing earnings or guidance.

### Fed, oil and rates

**The Fed held rates, but dissent confirmed that inflation risk remains open.**

On July 29, the Fed held its target range at 3.50%–3.75%. Three members favored a 25-basis-point increase, and the statement again highlighted high inflation and energy-related supply shocks. August’s risk chain is conditional: higher oil could raise expected inflation, rate volatility and the cost of capital; easing crude prices would break that sequence.

### Korea and Asian technology

**Strong earnings and a fragile structure can coexist.**

Institutional material shows very strong earnings revisions in Korea, but exceptional concentration in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix. Leveraged products and their rebalancing amplified the move. The market can look cheap on near-term earnings and expensive on normalized earnings: the difference depends on how long extraordinary memory and semiconductor margins last.

### USD/COP

**The peso strengthened more than the global dollar alone can explain.**

TRM was COP 3,144.14 per dollar for August 1–3, while Banco de la República’s policy rate remained 12%. Oil and the rate differential can support the peso, but do not individually identify the contribution of policy, fiscal conditions, capital flows or corporate hedging. After a rapid move, a wide range is more prudent than linear extrapolation.

### Company selection

**Dispersion turned each earnings release into a valuation and financing test.**

Recent corporate reactions showed that a good headline is insufficient when positioning is crowded. Arista Networks’ August 4 earnings, Duolingo’s on August 5 and Cloudflare’s on August 6 are confirmed for the first week of August, all after the US close. Focus on sales, margins, guidance, returns on CapEx, balance sheets and whether the reaction persists over three to five sessions.

## Follow-up reading by asset

### VOO / S&P 500

**The index remains standing, but low correlation is doing part of the work.**

Classification: **functional**

- **What happened:** The S&P 500 absorbed violent rotation because the damage concentrated in factors and companies, rather than the entire market simultaneously. The index’s calm understates the difficulty of operating beneath the surface.
- **What changed:** Volatility began moving from individual stocks to indices and hedging demand increased. Several systematic strategies moved closer to levels that could force them to reduce exposure.
- **What I expect:** Base case: high dispersion, selective rebounds and corrections, with an index that remains functional if earnings and structural breadth hold.
- **What to watch:** Correlation, VIX, 20-, 50- and 200-day breadth, credit spreads, bank reactions to long-term rates and systematic-strategy selling levels.
- **Report reading:** VOO retains its core role, but a stable index does not eliminate concentration risk or the risk of a transition to more correlated selling.
- **Before / context:** AI and momentum leadership supported the index amid high concentration.
- **Now / change:** Internal rotation is extreme and low correlation still cushions the aggregate.
- **Next signals:** Earnings, breadth and credit will show whether the adjustment clears excesses or reaches the index.

### GLD / Gold

**The structural thesis improves before the technical signal does.**

Classification: **diversifier**

- **What happened:** The technical weakness described in July did not disappear as uncertainty rose. The dollar, real yields and positioning remain the first filters for gold’s tactical reading.
- **What changed:** The long-term case gained support from public debt, central-bank purchases and supply risk, but that thesis does not replace price confirmation.
- **What I expect:** It could regain prominence if real yields or the dollar fall and its technical structure improves. If long-term yields remain high, pressure could continue.
- **What to watch:** The 200-day moving average, real yields, DXY, central-bank buying and GLD flow pressure.
- **Report reading:** Distinguish structural diversification from a tactical entry: their horizons and signals differ.
- **Before / context:** GLD accumulated prolonged technical weakness during July.
- **Now / change:** The structural narrative improves, but prices have not confirmed it.
- **Next signals:** Real yields and recovery of the long-term moving average will be the main signals.

### EWJ / Japan

**Japan retains relative strength, but its leadership is more sensitive to technology, the yen and rates.**

Classification: **selective**

- **What happened:** Japan continues to offer a distinct reading within developed Asia. The global momentum correction also reached Japan and revealed greater dependence on technology, investment and AI.
- **What changed:** Factor volatility stopped being exclusively a US issue. Meanwhile, normalizing Japanese yields restore a more important role for the yen and monetary policy in returns.
- **What I expect:** Base case: relative strength with greater dispersion. Continuity requires defensible earnings, broader leadership and an orderly currency.
- **What to watch:** The yen, Japanese yields, breadth, technology concentration, earnings revisions and sustained regional momentum.
- **Report reading:** Separate stock returns from currency effects. EWJ includes yen exposure and is not equivalent to a currency-hedged Japan investment.
- **Before / context:** Japan had been showing a better relative tone within developed Asia.
- **Now / change:** The momentum correction exposes concentration and restores the importance of the yen and rates.
- **Next signals:** Earnings breadth and currency stability will determine whether leadership holds.

### FXI / China

**China held up better than its semiconductors, but still needs a broad-market catalyst.**

Classification: **tactical**

- **What happened:** On the day of the greatest pressure on Asian semiconductors, the broad Chinese index was much more resilient. The divergence indicates that the lithography announcement shocked the technology supply chain rather than triggering uniform liquidation across China.
- **What changed:** The announced DUV advance introduced a long-term industrial-substitution hypothesis, but commercial-scale production, industrial yields and EUV capability remain unproven.
- **What I expect:** Base case: a tactical reading dependent on policy, confidence, earnings and flows. Technology risk alone is not enough to turn FXI into sustained leadership.
- **What to watch:** Stimulus, growth, domestic confidence, foreign flows, DXY, Chinese market breadth and verifiable industrial semiconductor evidence.
- **Report reading:** Do not confuse broad-index resilience with validation of the whole technology chain, or low valuations with a confirmed trend.
- **Before / context:** FXI retained a contrarian reading based on underperformance and valuation.
- **Now / change:** The broad market holds up better than semiconductors but still lacks consistent leadership.
- **Next signals:** Policy, earnings and flows must confirm any more lasting improvement.

### BTC / ETH

**Crypto retains its high-beta role rather than offering a defensive hedge.**

Classification: **high beta**

- **What happened:** BTC and ETH remain sensitive to liquidity, the dollar and risk appetite. Technology dispersion and a higher cost of capital reduce the usefulness of reading crypto as an isolated narrative.
- **What changed:** Attention shifts from spot prices to the combination of flows, leverage and correlation with technology assets.
- **What I expect:** A better tone if momentum and VIX stabilize, DXY falls and flows return. Greater vulnerability if credit, rates and systematic selling become connected.
- **What to watch:** BTC and ETH spot, BTC ETF flows, DXY, VIX, technology and financing conditions.
- **Report reading:** Do not replace liquidity or defensive exposure with crypto; its role in this picture is to measure beta and liquidity.
- **Before / context:** Liquidity explained much of the appetite for crypto.
- **Now / change:** The market again demands discipline around the dollar and technology volatility.
- **Next signals:** Flows and risk stability will determine the quality of any rebound.

### Stockpicking

**The subsequent reaction matters more than getting the first move right.**

Classification: **selective**

- **What happened:** The recent earnings season punished several companies more than the prior implied move suggested. Without a reproducible timestamp for options, those comparisons serve as observations rather than definitive statistics.
- **What changed:** The question is no longer only who grows, but who finances that growth, preserves margins and converts CapEx into cash flow. Semiconductors, Korea, AI infrastructure and high beta can represent the same exposure despite appearing as different companies.
- **What I expect:** Nine companies have expected reporting windows between August 3 and August 11. A useful reading will combine the surprise, guidance and cumulative reaction over the following 3 to 5 sessions.
- **What to watch:** Sales, margins, guidance, returns on CapEx, balance sheets, financing, the immediately preceding implied move and the reaction after 3 and 5 sessions.
- **Report reading:** Do not confuse different companies with different factors: semiconductors, AI, high beta and Asian technology can duplicate the same exposure.
- **Before / context:** Momentum allowed several companies to share the same narrative.
- **Now / change:** Earnings and the cost of capital distinguish cash-generating businesses from valuation-dependent stories.
- **Next signals:** Post-earnings persistence will distinguish fundamental signals from the initial adjustment.

## Possible paths

Conditional scenarios for organizing monitoring; these are neither predictions nor recommendations.

### Drivers

#### Earnings and AI monetization

Sales, margins and returns on CapEx will determine which companies retain leadership.

#### The Fed, oil and long-term rates

Will determine whether financial conditions stabilize or tighten again.

#### Correlation, systematic strategies and USD/COP

Breadth, positioning and the dollar will show whether internal volatility reaches the index and emerging-market currencies.

### Scenarios

#### Base

Dispersion and rotation continue without a broad index breakdown. Earnings separate winners and losers.

#### Positive

Earnings justify part of AI spending, credit stabilizes, yields ease and breadth improves.

#### Adverse

Earnings disappoint, credit spreads widen, oil or rates rise, and recovering correlation triggers systematic selling.

### Long-term rates and banks

- **Category:** Rates and credit

- **Status:** Under pressure
- **What it tracks:** The thirty-year Treasury, real yields and bank reactions to higher yields.
- **Why it matters:** If high rates stop helping banks and begin weakening them, deleveraging risk increases.
- **Reading at publication:** Long-term yields remain a more important constraint on valuations and financing.
- **What would change it:** A sustained decline would ease pressure; new highs alongside weak banks would weaken the thesis.
- **Date:** July 31, 2026
- **Source:** BofA, market data and the report’s editorial reading.
- **Link:** [Follow Treasury real yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_real_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026)

### Correlation and breadth

- **Category:** Market structure

- **Status:** Under observation
- **What it tracks:** Realized and implied correlation and the percentage of stocks above their 20-, 50- and 200-day averages.
- **Why it matters:** Low correlation allows the index to absorb opposing company movements.
- **Reading at publication:** Correlation remains exceptionally low while short-term breadth deteriorates.
- **What would change it:** Rising correlation and VIX alongside deteriorating breadth would transmit the damage to the index.
- **Date:** July 31, 2026
- **Source:** Goldman Sachs, breadth data and the report’s editorial reading.
- **Link:** [Follow S&P 500 levels](https://www.luiguiherrera.com/en/quantitative-analysis?asset=SPY)
