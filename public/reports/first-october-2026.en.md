## General context

October begins with a clear contradiction: the calendar is favorable, but the market beneath the index has yet to keep pace.

Technology retains leadership and earnings expectations remain strong. AI investment shows no signs of slowing either. The problem is concentration: breadth has deteriorated since Jackson Hole and I still do not see a sufficiently broad recovery to consider it resolved.

The Fed has more room to pause in October, but long-term yields remain high. Less risk of another immediate increase does not mean the cost of capital has stopped mattering.

My Q4 reading remains constructive: I want more stocks to participate without leaders falling. If that happens, market quality improves. Otherwise, we remain too dependent on a few companies.

### Breadth, sentiment and flows

51% of the Russell 3000 experienced a maximum drawdown greater than 20% since June; that does not mean they remain 20% down today. MA200 readings have different cutoffs; there is no single comparable percentage. [A7–A8]

BofA Bull & Bear falls from 9.3 to 8.8 and remains high; CNN Fear & Greed is 31 (fear); AAII bears fall from 53.3% to 46.5% between September 16 and September 30. They measure different positioning, market conditions and opinions. Michigan ends September at 48.1. J.P. Morgan records an average +24.1% twelve-month S&P 500 return following nine historical troughs: identified retrospectively, they do not identify a bottom today. [A9–A10; B2]

ETFs receive average daily inflows of $3.276 billion in September versus $7.036 billion in June. Retail buying is below its twelve-month average, without implying net selling. Greater net equity issuance adds supply. [A11]

### The Fed and financing

October points to a pause at 3.75–4.00%, rather than a cut: FedWatch assigns 77.9% to that range and 22.1% to 4.00–4.25%; the latter is December’s modal range. Employment adds 29,000 payrolls, with unemployment at 4.2% and −60,000 revisions for July and August. The ten-year Treasury rebounded intraday toward 5.28% after the release. Both the level and speed of yields matter. [A6; A12; B1; B3]

The calendar helps; breadth must confirm.

## Possible paths

### Scenarios

#### Base path · resilience with incomplete recovery

Leaders deliver, yields stay high but orderly, and breadth stabilizes without fully recovering. Gains and corrections with dispersion. Support weakens if leaders also deteriorate.

#### Favorable path · laggards join in

RSP and IWM gain against SPY and more sectors participate. Technology delivers; energy and financing ease pressure. BTC broadens demand and ETH confirms against BTC.

#### Adverse path · weakness reaches leaders

Financing tightens or earnings disappoint without internal recovery. Weakness reaches leaders. Selective accumulation fails to absorb BTC selling; gold remains exposed to correction.

Three conditional paths through the next publication, without assigned probabilities.

### Methodology

- My levels frozen at October 2, 2026. Metrics are not updated without available data.
- Weekly return = (October 2 adjusted close / September 25 adjusted close − 1) × 100. Same [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SPY/history/) series and window for all instruments. Spreads calculated before rounding.
- Historical frequencies are not probabilities. Scenarios are conditional. QQQ is a reference against SPY and BTC rather than a core asset; no current correlation has been calculated.
