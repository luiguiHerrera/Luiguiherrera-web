# First report of July

AI, flows and concentration: a strong but more mechanical market

## Main thesis

The market retains a constructive bias, supported by artificial intelligence, technology, momentum, passive flows and retail participation. The same forces supporting prices also increase fragility: high concentration, very short-dated options activity and dependence on corporate earnings.

## Executive summary

- **VOO:** A constructive but concentrated core.
- **GLD:** A useful pause and a defensive role.
- **EWJ:** Relative strength in developed Asia.
- **FXI:** A tactical position without clear leadership.
- **BTC/ETH:** High beta, dependent on liquidity.
- **Stockpicking:** More relevant amid dispersion and lower correlation.

## Context by asset

### Broad market

**Risk appetite persists, but strength is concentrated.**

The market retains an uneven appetite for risk. Technology, AI, momentum and flows support the index, while high concentration makes it necessary to look beneath the surface.

### Flows and structure

**Flows continue to support prices ahead of the narrative.**

Flows into technology remain strong. Passive investment and ETFs act as structural buyers, retail participation remains active, and very short-dated options add to the market’s mechanics. In this environment, flows can move prices before the fundamental narrative does.

### AI and infrastructure

**AI is moving from narrative to measurable infrastructure.**

AI investment is moving from narrative to infrastructure. Data centers, chips, memory, energy, software, cybersecurity and industrials form one interconnected chain. Buying any company that mentions AI is not enough: earnings, margins, valuation and execution matter.

### Risk

**Concentration and options increase vulnerability to reversals.**

Concentration, stretched momentum, earnings sensitivity, 0DTE activity and contained but fragile volatility increase reversal risk. An abrupt change in flows would be the main warning signal.

## Follow-up reading by asset

### VOO / S&P 500

**The index remains strong, but increasingly depends on a handful of drivers.**

Classification: **constructive**

- **What happened:** The S&P 500 retains a constructive reading because flows, momentum, earnings expectations and technology keep pushing it forward. The issue is not market weakness, but highly concentrated strength.
- **What changed:** AI, ETFs, passive investment and retail activity mean prices can keep rising even when breadth fails to keep pace.
- **What I expect:** A constructive bias while flows and earnings remain supportive.
- **What to watch:** Concentration, VIX, breadth, earnings and SPX/JPM levels.
- **Report reading:** Keep it as the core market reference, without confusing a strong index with a healthy market beneath the surface.
- **Before / context:** Technology and earnings were already supporting the trend.
- **Now / change:** Flows sustain momentum amid high concentration.
- **Next signals:** Breadth and earnings will show whether the advance is broadening.

### GLD / Gold

**Gold is resting, but has not lost its place in the picture.**

Classification: **defensive**

- **What happened:** Gold is not leading 2026 like other risk assets, but it continues to serve a different purpose: diversification and defense against regime changes. Assets do not all have to gain at the same time to be useful.
- **What changed:** Risk appetite and technology have reduced its short-term prominence.
- **What I expect:** It could remain quiet while risk appetite dominates, but retains value if interest-rate, dollar, inflation or stress pressures rise.
- **What to watch:** The dollar, real yields, inflation, financial stress and liquidity.
- **Report reading:** Do not expect momentum-asset behavior from it; its role is different.
- **Before / context:** It had been acting as a diversifier against regime changes.
- **Now / change:** The market is more focused on growth, AI and risk beta.
- **Next signals:** The dollar, real yields and inflation will determine whether it regains prominence.

### EWJ / Japan

**Japan continues to show a better relative tone within developed Asia.**

Classification: **selective**

- **What happened:** Japan retains a stronger reading than other Asian markets. Beyond geographical diversification, it currently offers exposure to a region with better relative momentum.
- **What changed:** Recent Asian leadership favors Japan/Korea more than China.
- **What I expect:** It can continue to provide geographical diversification with momentum.
- **What to watch:** The yen, Japanese rates, global risk appetite and continued regional leadership.
- **Report reading:** Interesting while relative strength holds; losing momentum would be the signal to review.
- **Before / context:** Asia was not moving as a homogeneous bloc.
- **Now / change:** Japan retains a better relative tone than China.
- **Next signals:** Currency movements and global risk appetite will be key.

### FXI / China

**China remains a tactical story rather than a confirmed trend.**

Classification: **tactical**

- **What happened:** China may look attractive because it has lagged, but lagging does not mean an immediate opportunity. The market still lacks the leadership seen in Japan, Korea or US technology.
- **What changed:** Global capital continues to prefer regions with better momentum.
- **What I expect:** A tactical reading; a catalyst is needed.
- **What to watch:** Chinese macro data, policy, foreign flows and the dollar.
- **Report reading:** Do not confuse cheap with ready to rise.
- **Before / context:** Underperformance opened a relative-value perspective.
- **Now / change:** Leadership remains in other regions and themes.
- **Next signals:** A macro or flow catalyst is needed to change the reading.

### BTC / ETH

**Crypto remains high beta: it can accelerate, but also amplify stress.**

Classification: **high beta**

- **What happened:** BTC and ETH depend on liquidity, risk appetite and flows. They can regain strength when markets seek risk; when volatility rises, they often behave as high-beta assets.
- **What changed:** The crypto reading remains more closely tied to liquidity and flows than to an isolated narrative.
- **What I expect:** Better performance if risk appetite persists; vulnerability if technology cools or VIX rises.
- **What to watch:** ETF flows, the dollar, VIX, technology and financial conditions.
- **Report reading:** High-risk exposure, rather than a substitute for liquidity or a defensive hedge.
- **Before / context:** Liquidity and flows explained much of the tone.
- **Now / change:** Beta to technology and risk appetite remains high.
- **Next signals:** ETF flows and VIX will provide the first warning.

### Stockpicking

**Selection matters more when the market stops moving as one.**

Classification: **selective**

- **What happened:** Low correlation and high dispersion mean the index does not tell the whole story. Some companies capture the AI, infrastructure or earnings narrative; others lag even as the index rises.
- **What changed:** The opportunity shifts from buying the whole market to understanding which companies actually monetize the trend.
- **What I expect:** A better environment for active selection, particularly in AI, infrastructure, semiconductors, software, cybersecurity, industrials and selected financials.
- **What to watch:** Real earnings, valuation, margins, catalysts and excessive momentum.
- **Report reading:** Avoid chasing stretched names without a clear fundamental story.
- **Before / context:** The index could hide large differences between companies.
- **Now / change:** Dispersion makes selection more important.
- **Next signals:** Earnings, margins and catalysts will separate leadership from noise.

### Scenarios

#### Base

A constructive but selective market. Technology and flows sustain the trend, with episodes of volatility.

#### Bullish

Results validate earnings, technology inflows continue, volatility remains controlled and breadth improves.

#### Bearish

Reversing flows, a tougher Fed/rate backdrop, disappointing earnings or deteriorating breadth trigger profit-taking.
